What do these metrics mean?

SDNN — Total heart rate variability. Think of it as your heart's overall adaptability. Athletes and healthy young adults often have SDNN > 50ms. Values below 30ms warrant attention. This is the strongest HRV marker for depression in research literature.

RMSSD — Short-term, beat-to-beat variability controlled by your vagus nerve. This nerve is the main highway of your parasympathetic nervous system — it slows your heart, calms your body, and helps you recover from stress. RMSSD naturally declines with age.

pNN50 — How often your heart makes meaningful beat-to-beat adjustments (>50ms). A complementary measure to RMSSD. If pNN50 is low, your heart isn't making many moment-to-moment adjustments, which can indicate chronic stress or autonomic rigidity.

LF/HF — The balance between your sympathetic ("go") and parasympathetic ("slow") systems. This ratio is most useful for detecting sympathetic overdrive (anxiety, PTSD). It is not a reliable depression marker — many depressed individuals have normal LF/HF ratios. The absolute power in each band (LF power, HF power) is often more informative than the ratio.

Glucose (est.) — Estimated fasting blood glucose derived from PPG waveform features (heart rate, HRV, arterial stiffness indices). Uses a multi-feature linear model calibrated against published PPG-glucose studies (R² up to 0.96 in research settings). Normal fasting: <5.6 mmol/L (<100 mg/dL); pre-diabetic: 5.6–6.9; diabetic: ≥7.0. This is a screening estimate, not a replacement for a blood test.

Status labels (LOW / NORMAL / HIGH) compare your value against age- and sex-matched population norms from studies of 13,000+ healthy participants. "Low" means more than 1.5 standard deviations below the norm for your demographic.

How are these calculated?

Each condition has a known "autonomic signature" — a pattern of which HRV metrics are typically reduced or elevated, based on published meta-analyses:

Depression — Broad HRV reduction. SDNN is typically most affected (g = −0.87). RMSSD and HF power moderately reduced (g ≈ −0.51). LF/HF ratio usually normal. Source: Wu et al. (2023), meta-analysis of 43 studies.

Anxiety (GAD) — Mild to moderate HRV reduction, particularly in parasympathetic indices (RMSSD, HF). Some sympathetic dominance (↑ LF/HF). Less pronounced than depression.

PTSD — Broad HRV reduction with possible sympathetic dominance. One of the stronger HRV-psychiatric associations in the 2025 umbrella review (Translational Psychiatry, Nature).

Bipolar Disorder — Mild HRV reduction. May normalize during stable (euthymic) periods. Weaker and less consistent than unipolar depression.

Schizophrenia — Strong parasympathetic reduction (↓↓ RMSSD, ↓↓ HF). The most robust HRV finding across all psychiatric conditions in the umbrella review.

Combined HRV + Glucose approach — The screening uses both autonomic (HRV) and metabolic (estimated glucose) biomarkers. Published multimodal studies show combining modalities improves diagnostic accuracy significantly: HRV alone achieves ~64% accuracy for MDD classification, but combining HRV with metabolic markers reaches ~80% (Jin et al., 2017, Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry). Each feature is weighted by its disorder-specific effect size from meta-analyses.

The confidence percentage reflects how many standard deviations your combined autonomic-metabolic pattern deviates from age/sex norms, weighted by each feature's importance for that condition. This is a screening signal, not a probability of having the condition.

Academic references
HRV norms van den Berg et al. (2018) — Normal values of corrected heart-rate variability in 10-second electrocardiograms for all ages. Frontiers in Physiology. 13,943 healthy participants, ages 11 days to 91 years. First comprehensive lifespan HRV reference ranges. Used for age/sex normalization of SDNN and RMSSD.
HRV norms Ortega et al. (2024) — "The Pulse of Singapore." Applied Psychophysiology & Biofeedback. 2,143 healthy participants, ages 10–89. Population norms: RMSSD ≈ 42 ms, SDNN ≈ 52 ms (5-min recordings). Used for pNN50 normative ranges.
Depression Wu et al. (2023) — Heart rate variability status at rest in adult depressed patients: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. 43 case-control studies, 2,359 depressed vs 3,547 controls. SDNN Hedge's g = −0.87 (largest effect). Used for depression autonomic signature weights.
All disorders Translational Psychiatry (2025) — Heart rate variability in mental disorders: an umbrella review of meta-analyses. Nature. 21 systematic reviews, 442 primary studies, 34,625 participants across 19 mental disorders. Classifies evidence as convincing/suggestive/weak. Used for disorder signatures and confidence thresholds.
Smartphone PPG Cajal et al. (2025) — Evaluation of Stress Response Using Smartphone PPG for Anxiety and Depression Monitoring. IEEE. 79 participants (22 patients, 57 controls). Validated smartphone PPG against medical-grade device: r ≥ 0.96. First SCPPG study with clinical anxiety/depression populations.
Smartphone PPG Liu et al. (2020) — Happiness at Your Fingertips: Assessing Mental Health with Smartphone Photoplethysmogram-Based Heart Rate Variability Analysis. Telemedicine and e-Health. 93 participants. 4-minute fingertip video. Higher HRV = higher well-being; lower HRV = depression and anxiety.
Pulse waveform Kaizu et al. (2026) — Assessing Mental Health and Emotional States by Using Smartphone Photoplethysmography–Based Digital Pulse Waveform Analysis. JMIR mHealth and uHealth. 127 participants. 7 pulse waveform features (F/A, V0, reflection index, crest time) associated with depression and anxiety.
Suicidal ideation Khandoker et al. (2017) — Suicidal Ideation Is Associated with Altered Variability of Fingertip Photo-Plethysmogram Signal in Depressed Patients. Frontiers in Physiology. 61 participants. Tone-entropy analysis of fingertip PPG achieved 93.33% accuracy for suicidal ideation classification.
Wearable PPG Lyu et al. (2025) — Wearable Photoplethysmography-based Bipolar Disorder Detection. CSAI 2024 (ACM). Wavelet transform + SVM on PPG from smartwatch. 86.5% accuracy distinguishing mania, bipolar depression, and healthy states.
Multi-disorder G-PsychSW (2025) — Generalizable Psychiatric Screening from Wearable: Causal Disentanglement of Multi-Channel Biosignals. IEEE BIBM. PPG + GSR + skin temperature. 80.16% accuracy for 4-class screening (depression, anxiety, bipolar, healthy). Leave-one-subject-out validation.
Signal processing Cho et al. (2018) — Instant Automated Inference of Perceived Mental Stress through Smartphone PPG and Thermal Imaging. JMIR Mental Health. Combined smartphone camera PPG with low-cost thermal camera for stress detection in 17 participants over 20-second measurements.
PPG-glucose Chinchanikar & Dale (2025) — Multimodal Approach for Non-Invasive Blood Glucose Estimation Using Fingertip Video. MMEP. 243 subjects. Hybrid ResNet + handcrafted PPG features. R² = 0.88, MAE = 14.50 mg/dL. Bland-Altman >90% agreement with reference.
PPG-glucose Raju et al. (2022) — SmartPPG-Glucose: non-invasive glucose from smartphone PPG. 93 subjects. DNN with MIC feature selection on 34 PPG features. R² = 0.96. Foundation for the PPG-to-glucose feature mapping used in MentalTap.
PPG-glucose Sridevi et al. (2025) — Noninvasive estimation of blood glucose and HbA1c using Quantum Machine Learning. Elsevier. 136 subjects. Quantum SVM on 45 PPG features. 89.3% accuracy for glucose, 96.3% for HbA1c.
Metabolic Wong et al. (2026) — Depressive symptom severity and metabolic disturbances in MDD and bipolar disorders: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. 28 studies, 22,897 participants. Fasting glucose SMD = 0.30 in depression. Insulin resistance associated with anhedonia, sleep disturbances, and suicidal ideation.
Multimodal Jin et al. (2017) — Diagnosis of MDD by combining multimodal information from heart rate dynamics and serum proteomics using machine-learning. Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry. HRV alone: 64% accuracy; HRV + metabolic proteomics: 80.1%. Demonstrates value of combining autonomic + metabolic biomarkers.
